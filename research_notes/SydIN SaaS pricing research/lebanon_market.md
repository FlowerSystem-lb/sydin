# Lebanese market for small-business inventory, POS and accounting software: what shops and depots pay (2023–2026)

> **Method note for the report writer.** The network proxy blocked direct page fetches (WebFetch) to nearly every relevant domain: worldbank.org, whish.money, lorientlejour.com, the vendor sites and auditfirmslebanon.com. Every finding below therefore comes from **web-search result extracts**, which quote the pages but can miss context. Each claim still carries the URL of the page it came from. Treat any number marked "vendor claim" or "directory listing" as unverified. No interviews or primary price quotes were possible. Today's date is 9 Oct 2026.

## 1. Which POS, ERP, inventory and accounting systems do Lebanese shops, depots and wholesalers use, and at what price?

### Takeaway
The Lebanese market splits into three tiers:
- **Cheap one-time desktop software and hardware bundles:** about $100–$600, sold on OLX and Facebook.
- **Local POS and ERP vendors:** quote-based. Most do not publish prices, and many sell lifetime licenses.
- **A newer group of cloud subscriptions:** about $15–$99/month.

International systems (SAP B1, Sage, QuickBooks, Odoo) are sold through partners and quotes only, priced per user. They are aimed at firms with 10+ users, not micro shops. Almost no Lebanese vendor publishes a clear price list.

### Inferences
- The realistic local price ceiling SydIN competes with for a micro shop or depot is **$100–$600 one-time** (OLX software or a Hamster-style bundle). The cloud floor is **$15/month** (YellowPOS). Mid-market cloud ERPs sit at **$35–$99/month** (Managely, MAPOS), and Odoo, QuickBooks and Zoho run **$20–$90/month**.
  - SydIN Standard at **$9/month ($90/year)** is below every Lebanese cloud price found.
  - SydIN Pro at **$19/month** is about the same as YellowPOS's entry price.
- On an annual basis, SydIN Standard ($90/year) costs about as much as one cheap OLX license ($100–$150) every year. A buyer used to "pay once" will make exactly that comparison.
- Several Lebanese competitors (YellowPOS, AR POS, MAPOS, Dokani) advertise **USD + LBP dual currency** and **offline operation during power/internet cuts**. These are table stakes in local marketing, and SydIN would be compared on them.
- Most local vendors sell POS (checkout) plus accounting. Few are positioned as a **phone-first inventory/depot tool with a camera scanner**. SydIN's differentiation is real, but buyers may see it as "incomplete" if it lacks sales and invoicing.

### Gaps
- No vendor-published Lebanon price list was found for BIM POS, Omega, Visual Dolphin, LIBRA, Software Age, Galoper, Cubix, AR POS, Dokani, SAP B1, Sage or the Odoo partners. All are "request a quote".
- No Facebook-page price lists could be read directly (fetch blocked). Hamster's $599 is the only Facebook-sourced figure, and it is undated.
- No evidence was found on which systems **phone-accessory shops or wholesalers specifically** use.
- The OLX listings may include resold or unlicensed copies; their legitimacy cannot be verified.
- Searches in Arabic ("برنامج محاسبة لبنان", "برنامج مستودعات لبنان") returned no useful vendor price results.

## 2. Do Lebanese SMBs prefer one-time licenses over subscriptions? Evidence of pirated software, or of Excel and paper as the main competitor?

### Takeaway
The evidence is indirect but consistent:
- **One-time licenses are still the dominant local offer.** POPs, Omega, Hamster, Smart POS and the OLX listings all sell this way, and subscription vendors explicitly market against "upfront licensing".
- **Piracy was historically high:** 69% of software was unlicensed in 2017, the last data point.
- **Digital adoption among SMEs is low.** No Lebanon survey measures Excel or paper notebook use directly.

### Cited Findings
- POPs LEB markets a "lifetime license" (pay once, keep forever) and claims ~80% of POS vendors charge subscriptions instead. Vendor claim. — [POPs LEB FAQ](https://popsleb.com/faq)
- Smart POS Software markets "Own It Forever. No Monthly Fees", a "pay-once lifetime license", with templated Lebanon/Beirut pages. Its origin is unclear and it may not be Lebanese. — [Smart POS Software](https://smartpossoftware.com/pages/smart-pos-software/); [Smart POS Lebanon tag page](https://smartpossoftware.com/pages/tag/lebanon/)
- YellowPOS positions its "$15/month" subscription against Omega's "upfront licensing", which shows the incumbent model is one-time. — [YellowPOS blog](https://yellow-pos.com/blog/best-pos-system-lebanon-2026)
- OLX accounting/stock software sells at a one-time $100–$150, negotiable. — [OLX Lebanon accounting listings](https://www.olx.com.lb/en/services/q-accounting/)
- **Piracy:**
  - The BSA put Lebanon's unlicensed software rate at **69% in 2017** (37th highest of 111 countries, 6th of 15 Arab countries), down from 70% in 2015 and 72% in 2009. Commercial value of losses: **$61m in 2017**. Older data; no 2020s figure found. — [Byblos Bank Lebanon This Week 540](https://www.byblosbank.com/common/economic-research-new/lebanon-this-week/lebanon-this-week-540/lebanons-piracy-rate-at-69-in-2017-37th-highest-globally-and-sixth-highest-among-arab-countries)
  - Lebanon created a Cyber Crime and IP Rights Bureau in 2006, which ran spot checks and market sweeps. Old data. — [WIPO Magazine 2005/2006 country focus](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/wipo-magazine/articles/country-focus-meeting-ip-challenges-in-lebanon-34963)
- **Digital adoption:**
  - "Only 20% of SMEs indicated that they had invested in digitalization as part of their crisis management strategy." Academic study; sample unclear. — [ResearchGate: Impact of Lebanon's economic collapse on commercial SMEs](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387556425_THE_IMPACT_OF_LEBANON'S_ECONOMIC_COLLAPSE_ON_COMMERCIAL_SMES)
  - 2024 Global Findex data for Lebanon (individuals, not businesses): 94.9% reported recent internet use but only **21.8%** made or received a digital payment. "Habit" was the top reason for paying cash only in stores (61.5%). — [SSRN: Digital Payment Adoption in Lebanon (2024 Findex)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7488798)
- **Excel:** A Lebanese vendor guide claims a business switching "from Excel to a real accounting platform usually saves 20 to 40 hours per month". This is marketing, but it shows vendors treat Excel as the incumbent. — [Voxire accounting software Lebanon 2026](https://voxire.com/blog/accounting-software-lebanon-smes-2026/)
- **Offline and outages:** Local vendors stress offline-first operation because of power and internet outages (Dokani, POPs, MAPOS). — [Dokani on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dokani.app&hl=en_US); [MAPOS restaurant POS Lebanon 2025](https://www.mapos.tech/en/blog/restaurant-pos-systems-lebanon-2025)

### Inferences
- A Lebanese shop owner's mental model is likely "software = a one-time purchase of ~$100–$600, possibly cracked or bundled free with hardware". A monthly fee will face "why pay forever?" pushback. The **annual plan ($90/year)** or a **"lifetime" / multi-year prepaid option** may be easier to sell than monthly. This is an estimate, not a measured preference.
- Cash habits (61.5% cite habit) and low SME digitalization (20%) suggest the real competitor for a phone-accessory shop or depot is **paper notebooks, WhatsApp photos and Excel**, not other software. A free tier is the right entry point. This is an inference; there is no direct survey.
- High piracy means a desktop license is easy to copy. A **cloud, account-based app is harder to pirate**, which is a structural advantage for SydIN, but only if the subscription value is obvious.

### Gaps
- No Lebanon-specific survey found on the share of shops using Excel, paper notebooks (دفتر) or no system at all.
- No 2020s piracy data for Lebanon (the BSA's last global survey with Lebanon data is 2017/2018).
- No survey found that directly measures one-time vs subscription preference among Lebanese SMBs.

## 3. Economic context 2024–2026: the cash economy, recovery, the wars, wages, and subscription anchors for willingness to pay

### Takeaway
After a ~40% cumulative GDP collapse (2019–2024), the economy has stabilized:
- Lebanon is **de facto dollarized and cash-heavy**: ~46% of GDP in cash in 2022, with the pound held at 89,500/USD since mid-2023.
- **2025 was the best year since 2019**, with growth of +3.5% to +4.2% depending on the World Bank edition.
- **The March 2026 escalation reversed this.** The World Bank (Aug 2026) projects **−6.4% GDP in 2026**, 17.5% inflation and 1.2 million displaced.
- The minimum wage is about **$312/month**. Consumer digital subscriptions in Lebanon cost **$4–$10/month**, and business internet sits at **$25–$100+/month**.

### Cited Findings

**GDP and the macro picture**
- 2024 real GDP contracted **7.1%**, bringing the cumulative decline since 2019 to "nearly 40%". The Spring 2025 projection for 2025 growth was +4.7%. — [World Bank press release, 19 Jun 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/06/19/fragile-stabilization-fuels-growth-in-lebanon)
- Winter 2025 LEM ("A Fragile Rebound", 22 Jan 2026): 2025 real GDP grew **3.5%** (revised down from 4.7%), driven by private consumption, remittances, tourism, and real estate and construction. — [World Bank press release, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/01/22/lebanon-economic-rebound-marks-cautious-recovery-amidst-progress-on-reforms)
- Coverage of the August 2026 LEM reports 2025 growth of **4.2%**, the "strongest growth since the onset of the financial crisis in 2019". This conflicts with the 3.5% from January 2026 and likely reflects a later revision. — [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-906322)
- LEM "A Conflict-Torn Economy" (21 Aug 2026):
  - Real GDP is projected to **contract 6.4% in 2026**, 10.4 points below a no-conflict scenario.
  - Inflation is forecast to rise to **17.5%**, and about **1.2 million people** were displaced.
  - Causes cited: "the collapse in tourism, weaker consumption, disrupted supply chains, heightened insecurity, and prolonged displacement" after the **March 2026 escalation**.
  - The 2025 fiscal surplus was 3.9% of GDP.

  — [World Bank press release, 21 Aug 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/08/21/renewed-conflict-derails-lebanon-s-fragile-economic-recovery); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/22/world-bank-projects-war-hit-lebanons-economy-to-contract-by-6-4-percent)
- Economy Minister Amer Bisat expected a **7%** contraction in 2026. — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/08/21/world-bank-projects-lebanons-gdp-to-contract-by-64-per-cent-in-2026/); [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1545292/world-bank-sees-lebanese-gdp-shrinking-by-more-than-6-in-2026.html)

### Inferences
- **$9/month ≈ 2.9% of one minimum-wage salary (~$312).** For a shop owner (who usually earns more than staff), it is roughly the price of a Netflix Standard plan or a basic Ogero line. That puts it within the "small recurring expense" band, but it is not trivial in a war year.
- A business that accepts **$100+/month for Starlink** or **$20–90/month for accounting** shows that dollar-denominated recurring B2B spend exists in Lebanon. Those are likely larger firms than SydIN's target, though.
- **Timing risk:** SydIN launches into a 2026 contraction (−6.4% to −7%) with 17.5% inflation and mass displacement, especially in the South, Bekaa and southern suburbs. Expect slower paid conversion in 2026 and a recovery-dependent upside for 2027. This is an estimate.
- Pricing in **USD** fits the market: the economy is de facto dollarized, the rate is stable, and every vendor found prices in USD.

### Gaps
- No reliable data on **typical shop-owner income** or small-retailer margins in Lebanon.
- No survey of how much SMEs spend monthly on software or subscriptions.
- No current (2025–2026) Ogero, Alfa or touch price sheet; the 2023 figures are the latest found.
- No confirmed Lebanon Anghami price, and no confirmed launched Starlink retail price.
- No World Bank cash-economy estimate after 2022.

## 4. Payment methods (Whish Money, OMT, BOB Finance, cash, USDT, cards): fees, and can a business collect recurring payments this way?

### Takeaway
- **Cash USD dominates.** Wallets and money-transfer networks (Whish, OMT) replaced banks for most transfers.
- **Whish** is the clearest rail for B2B collection: free wallet-to-wallet, 1% cash-out, and a merchant gateway (Whish Pay) at ~1%.
- **Card use is limited** to "fresh USD" cards and urban merchants.
- **No evidence of native recurring or auto-debit billing** was found on Whish or OMT. Collection is per payment (a link or manual transfer), which matches SydIN's manual-activation model.

### Cited Findings

**Whish Money**
- Published fees:
  - Whish-to-Whish transfers are **free**.
  - Cash-out over the counter or at an ATM costs **1%**.
  - Funding a wallet by card costs **3%**, and a **payment link costs 3%**.
  - Local LBP transfers cost 1% (minimum LBP 50,000).
  - The individual account balance limit is **USD 3,000**.

  — [Whish Money Fees & Commissions](https://www.whish.money/fees-commissions)
- **Whish Pay** (merchant gateway):
  - Merchant cost is **1%** for websites (**2% on Shopify**), with "no separate cash-out fee" for merchants.
  - **Corporate account max balance: USD 30,000.**
  - In-store "Scan to Pay" by QR code.
  - Licensed under BdL Resolution No. 19/21/22 (10 Aug 2022).

  — [leb.business: Whish Money vs OMT for Lebanese merchants](https://leb.business/en/article/whish-money-vs-omt-which-pays-better-for-lebanese-merchants); [Waradana guide](https://waradana.com/english/article/270970-whish-money-a-comprehensive-guide-to-using-lebanons-digital-financial-platform)
- Whish partnered with **Mastercard Move** (Aug 2025) for cross-border payments. — [Reuters via TradingView](https://es.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com%2C2025-08-21%3Anewsml_Zaw60fdfD%3A0-pressr-whish-money-collaborates-with-mastercard-move-to-bring-seamless-cross-border-payments-to-lebanon)
- Whish receive limit via Taptap Send: **$5,000/month**. Recipients pay no cash-out fee. — [Taptap Send help](https://help.taptapsend.com/en/receiving-countries/how-can-i-send-money-to-lebanon)
- Whish has 1,000–1,400+ cash in/out locations (sources differ). — [leb.business](https://leb.business/en/article/whish-money-vs-omt-which-pays-better-for-lebanese-merchants)

**OMT**
- OMT has **1,400+ locations** and "quotes merchant pricing privately". — [leb.business](https://leb.business/en/article/whish-money-vs-omt-which-pays-better-for-lebanese-merchants)
- Its local (intra) transfer price list, valid from 1 Nov 2025, is in LBP brackets: amounts up to LBP 5,000,000 cost a **LBP 50,000** fee (≈ $0.56). — [OMT Intra price list](https://omt.com.lb/en/services/money-transfer/omt-intra-local-money-transfer/price-list)
- OMT ran a zero-fee campaign on local LBP transfers from June 2023, extended to 31 Dec 2023. — [OMT news](https://informative.omt.com.lb/en/news/omt-extends-zero-fees-campaign-on-local-lbp-transfers-until-years-end)
- Inbound Western Union payouts via OMT are in cash USD with a **2% cash-handling fee**. — [OMT Western Union APN](https://informative.omt.com.lb/en/services/money-transfer/western-union-apn)

**How merchants actually collect**
- Lebanese online merchants "use e-wallet apps (Whish, Suyool, OMT) to manually generate payment links for each customer", which creates friction and trust issues. — [Shopify Community thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/what-payment-methods-can-i-use-for-my-store-in-lebanon/292169)
- A Lebanese agency's gateway comparison puts Whish at 1–2% with same- or next-day settlement. Vendor source. — [Voxire payment gateways Lebanon 2026](https://voxire.com/blog/payment-gateways-lebanon-ecommerce-2026/)

**Cards**
- Under a BdL circular, **local (pre-crisis) USD cards cannot be used at POS or online**; only **"Fresh USD" cards** can. — [BLOM Fresh USD acceptance](https://www.blomretail.com/fresh-usd-acceptance)
- Byblos Visa Fresh debit limits: $5,000/day, $25,000/week. — [Byblos Bank Visa Fresh](https://www.byblosbank.com/personal/cards/debit/visa-fresh)
- Card acceptance is concentrated in hotels, restaurants, supermarkets and urban areas, "less so in smaller businesses". — [ATM Fee Saver guide 2025](https://explore.atmfeesaver.com/blog/money-currency-in-lebanon/)
- Only **21.8%** of Lebanese adults made or received a digital payment (2024 Findex). — [SSRN Findex analysis](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7488798)

**USDT**
- Chainalysis states that its data does not capture OTC, hawala or cash-crypto-shop activity, so Lebanese USDT use is undercounted. — [Chainalysis 2025 Geography of Crypto](https://www.chainalysis.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/the-2025-geography-of-crypto-report-release.pdf)
- Claims that USDT is a "de facto medium of exchange" in Beirut, that street vendors accept it, and that Whish integrates Binance P2P all come from blogs or aggregators. They are unverified. — [deepbrief21 Substack](https://deepbrief21.substack.com/p/7-countries-using-stablecoins-to); [The Beiruter on Binance](https://www.thebeiruter.com/article/lebanon-meets-with-binance-on-crypto-regulation/1204)

### Inferences
- For SydIN, the **cheapest collection rail is Whish wallet-to-wallet (free)**. Customers paying from their own Whish balance cost SydIN nothing until cash-out (1%). A $9 payment via OMT local transfer costs the sender ≈ $0.56 in LBP. Both are viable for **manual, per-period payments**, not automatic recurring debit.
- Because collection is manual and not automatic, **annual prepayment** ($90/$190) reduces collection effort, churn-by-forgetting and per-payment friction. This is a strong operational argument for pushing annual plans in Lebanon.
- The individual Whish balance cap is $3,000 and the corporate cap is $30,000. If SydIN scales, it would need a Whish **corporate** account.
- A card gateway would reach only "fresh USD" cardholders, a minority of small shop owners. Low priority for the Lebanese segment.

### Gaps
- **BOB Finance:** no fee or merchant data found.
- No evidence found of any **recurring/subscription (auto-debit) feature** on Whish, OMT or Suyool. Whether one exists is unconfirmed, and should be asked of Whish business support directly.
- OMT merchant collection fees are not published.
- No data on the share of SMB owners holding Whish accounts or fresh-USD cards.
- USDT shop-level usage has no reliable measurement.

## 5. Market size: registered companies and SMEs, retail shops, phone and accessory shops, wholesalers and depots

### Takeaway
No reliable, current official count exists. The most-cited figure is **"over 225,000 MSMEs"** (undated IRC/Building Markets study, likely pre-crisis), and the UNDP says MSMEs are **~90% of the economy**, concentrated in Beirut and Mount Lebanon. There is **no count of phone-accessory shops, wholesalers or depots**. The last full establishment census by CAS dates from **2004–2005**.

### Cited Findings
- "Over **225,000** micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) in Lebanon, two-thirds of which are concentrated in … Beirut and Mount Lebanon … a large proportion … operate informally." Undated, likely pre-2019. — [IRC & Building Markets SME market overview](https://www.rescue.org/sites/default/files/document/656/ircandbuildingmarketssmemarketoverviewlebanonrelease.pdf)
- MSMEs are "around 90 per cent of the Lebanese economy". 15% of surveyed businesses closed permanently after the 2024 war. — [UNDP policy brief, Jul 2025](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2025-07/policy_brief_socioeconomic-impacts-lebanon-2024-war.pdf)
- A Ministry of Industry census counted 8,200 factories, 95% of them SMEs (4,700 registered). Industry only; 2018 publication. — [OECD MENA 2018](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-mediterranean-middle-east-and-north-africa-2018_9789264304161-en/full-report/component-17.html)
- The World Bank Enterprise Survey for Lebanon (2019) sampled **532 firms**, 129 of them in wholesale and retail. It is a sample, not a count. — [Enterprise Surveys Lebanon 2019 profile](https://espanol.enterprisesurveys.org/content/dam/enterprisesurveys/documents/country/Lebanon-2019.pdf)
- CAS's last census of buildings, dwellings and establishments was in **2004–2005**. — [ISTAT/CAS presentation on Lebanon's statistical system](https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/National_statistical_system_in_Lebanon.pdf)
- The Commercial Register (Ministry of Justice) holds registered entities, but no public total was found. — [Sayari: Lebanon Commercial Register](https://learn.sayari.com/lebanon-commercial-register/)
- Private directories give inconsistent counts: 33,095 companies (Sept 2026) vs 132,956 indexed businesses. Unreliable. — [CompanyData Lebanon](https://companydata.com/directory/business-directory-lebanon/); [DiscoLike Lebanon](https://discolike.com/company-stats/countries/lebanon/)

### Inferences
- A rough top-down estimate (an estimate, not a fact): of ~225k MSMEs, retail and wholesale is plausibly the largest single sector. Even if only a few thousand are depots or stock-heavy shops reachable by a phone-first English app, the **serviceable market in Lebanon is in the low thousands of businesses**. At $9–$19/month that is a small revenue pool, so Lebanon works best as a **launch and validation market** rather than the main revenue market.
- The post-2024 closures (15%) and the 2026 escalation mean any pre-crisis count overstates today's active businesses.

### Gaps
- No count of **mobile phone and accessory shops**, **wholesalers** or **depots** in Lebanon.
- No current CAS establishment census, and no published total from the Commercial Register.
- No CCIA-BML (Beirut Chamber) membership numbers found.
- No IDAL SME statistics found.

## 6. Lebanese startups that sold SaaS to local SMBs, and their price points (USD)

### Takeaway
Local SaaS selling to Lebanese SMBs exists but is young, and most of it is **quote-based**. The few published prices cluster at **$15–$99/month** (YellowPOS, Managely, MAPOS), plus a directory "from $29" for BIM POS. No Lebanese SMB-SaaS company with disclosed paying-customer numbers or revenue was found.

### Cited Findings
- **YellowPOS** (Lebanese, browser-based POS): from **$15/month**, 1-month free trial, no card. Vendor claim. — [YellowPOS blog](https://yellow-pos.com/blog/best-pos-system-lebanon-2026)
- **MAPOS / I-MAD Technology** (Lebanese ERP): from **$49/month** or **$99/month** (conflicting claims on its own blog); CRM at $29/user/month; Starter, Professional and Enterprise tiers. — [MAPOS ERP Lebanon 2025](https://www.mapos.tech/en/blog/best-erp-software-lebanon-2025); [MAPOS accounting 2026](https://www.mapos.tech/en/blog/best-accounting-software-lebanon-mena-2026)

### Inferences
- Every published local cloud price found is **at or above $15/month**, so SydIN's Standard plan ($9/month) is the **cheapest paid inventory/POS-adjacent cloud offer found in Lebanon**. Pro ($19) is about equal to YellowPOS's entry tier.
  - This suggests SydIN is **not overpriced for Lebanon**.
  - The bigger risk is the one-time and cracked-software mindset and the 2026 downturn, not the price level.
- Free trials (14 days to 1 month, no card) are the norm among local cloud vendors. SydIN's permanent Free tier (50 items, 1 depot) is more generous than the local norm.
- Several Lebanese vendors publish no prices and rely on "request a quote" via WhatsApp or phone. Clear, public USD pricing could be a differentiator for SydIN, but it also makes SydIN easier to compare against cheap one-time OLX software.

### Gaps
- No Lebanese SMB SaaS company found with disclosed customer counts, conversion rates or ARPU.
- No Berytech, Flat6Labs, Executive Magazine or BusinessNews.com.lb article found with SaaS price points for Lebanese SMB tools. Search results did not surface them, and direct fetch was blocked.
- Several vendors' pricing pages could not be loaded (fetch blocked), so the actual current tiers for YellowPOS, AR POS, Dokani and Galoper are unverified.
